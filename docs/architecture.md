# Ta Da architecture

How the app is built. The file format and writing rules are in `docs/file-format.md`; the design system is in `docs/design/design-framework.md`.

## Layers

- **TaDaCore** (`Packages/TaDaCore`, local package): the model, parsing, surgical writes, the index, queries and storage. Foundation, `MarkdownEngineSyntax` (the Live editor package's grammar) and Yams 6.2.2 only; no UI.
- **TaDa** (`TaDa/`, app target): SwiftUI views over an `@Observable` `AppModel`. macOS 26 and iOS 26 minimum; on the Mac, App Sandbox with user-selected read/write, Calendars and outgoing connections. The model, theme, live editor, folder handling and the document's title, body, banner and footer build for both; the Mac's views are behind `#if os(macOS)` and the iPhone's are in `TaDa/iOS/`.
- **MarkdownEngine** (package `swift-markdown-engine-extended`, pinned to an exact `-live.<n>` tag): the Live editor, a fork of swift-markdown-engine under Apache-2.0, for macOS and iOS. Parsing, styling, layout, input and the text-delegate path are shared; `Platform/Platform.swift` names the AppKit and UIKit types, and `TextView/iOS/` holds the iOS editor. Its `FORK.md` lists the changes from upstream. Its package also declares HighlighterSwift and SwiftMath, for upstream's code-block and LaTeX products; `Package.resolved` pins them within the fork's `from:` ranges (a re-resolve may move them, harmlessly) and Xcode fetches them, but Ta Da doesn't link or build them.

The workspace `TaDa.xcworkspace` holds the project and TaDaCore; the test plan `TaDa.xctestplan` runs TaDaTests, TaDaCoreTests and TaDaUITests.

`TaDa/Acknowledgements.txt` ships in the app bundle with the notices and licences of the linked packages (the editor fork, Yams and its libyaml, and Sparkle) and of the bundled themes' palettes (Ayu, TokyoNight, Catppuccin), copied from their own files; Settings ▸ About lists each package's name, URL and licence from it (`Acknowledgement`).

## Runtime

- **Launch**: resolve the folder's security-scoped bookmark, scan it recursively off the main actor, parse every file into a `VaultIndex`. A change to a file patches only that item's entries in the index's title and link maps (tasks by project title, projects and tasks by area title, and files by the image filenames they embed, which `embedders(of:)` reads with counts). `parent(of:)` is a task's project, else its area, or a project's area; `area(of:)` reaches a task's area through its project. `AppModel` works out today's groups (`grouped`), the Agenda's period, the Loose list, the loose projects and the areas with their projects from the index's `Queries` (which take an injected `today`) once per change, and views read those; a refresh applies all its changed files as one change. Each query applies a per-item test (`isInToday`, `upcomingEntries(of:after:)`, `isLoose`), which `FileInspector` uses for one file, and `progress(of:)` counts any project's tasks.
- **Agenda**: `AppModel.agendaSelection` is `.day(LocalDate)` or `.week(ISOWeek)` and opens on today; when the day changes, a selection on the old today moves to the new one. `agendaMonthOpen` says whether the strip shows the month. Each change of either, and each index change, runs `updateAgenda()`: a day's tasks come from `Queries.agendaDay(_:today:)` into `agendaTasks` (today's rule for today, else the tasks due or at their deadline that day, open or Done), a week's from `agendaWeek(_:)` into `agendaWeekEntries` (one `AgendaEntry` per date), `agendaOccurrences` from `occurrences(from:through:today:)`, and `agendaMarks` from `dayMarks(from:through:today:names:)` for `agendaMarksRange`, the days shown plus the page before and after. A `DayMarks` has `hasOpenTasks` (an open task is due or at its deadline that day, or a repeating task's occurrence falls on it) and `hasOpenBoxes` (the day's daily note has unchecked boxes, from `VaultIndex.openCheckboxCount`, which reads the index's `checkboxes`); editing a daily note's boxes updates the marks in place. The strip draws them as dots (`AgendaDots`): a ring in `due` for open boxes, a `due` dot for open tasks, a `muted` dot for an event or reminder; a week number is drawn in `link` when its week note exists. `agendaRange` is the selected week, or every week of the anchor's month when the month is open. `stepAgenda(by:)` moves a day, or a week when a week is selected; `showAgendaToday()` returns to today. A new task in the Agenda, and a task dropped on its sidebar row, is due on `agendaDueDay`: the selected day, or today when a week is selected. The badge still counts today's open tasks (`todayCount`).
- **Daily notes**: `DailyNoteNames(dailyFolder:weekFolder:)` in TaDaCore gives a date's or an ISO week's path and reads a path back, matching the folder in any case; `LocalDate.isoWeek`, `ISOWeek` (`firstDay` Monday, `days`, `adding(weeks:)`) and `isoWeeksOfMonth` carry the weeks. `VaultIndex.dailyNote(for:names:)` and `weekNote(for:names:)` find a note by its exact path among the index's untyped files, so a renamed note isn't found. `AppModel` keeps the folders, the Week notes switch and the two templates as settings (`dailyFolder`, `weekFolder`, `weekNotes`, `dailyTemplate`, `weekTemplate`, in the folder's settings file). `note(for:)` gives a period's note and `openOrCreateNote(for:)` selects it or, only when asked, creates it (`NewFile.dailyNote(template:today:)`: a note's frontmatter, then the template's body) in the existing folder's own case, creating the folders and never numbering a name. The Agenda's note cards read their first checkbox lines through `boxLines(of:)`, cached by note until it changes (`cachedBoxLines`).
- **Tags**: `PropertyReader` reads `tags:` and `BodyScanner` the body's `#tags` (`MarkdownFacts`, as the spec reads them); `Item.tags` merges them as `TagUse`s, each marked `inFrontmatter` or not. `VaultIndex` keeps a tag path → paths map, patched per item and leaving out logged files, with each tag spelled as its carrier with the first path does. `TagQueries` builds the tag tree (`TagNode`s with rolled-up counts, parents carried by no file added), `items(tagged:)` with children, `untagged()`, `tagBrowser(_:)` (tagged areas and projects, open tasks in the Agenda's groups with Loose last, Completed, Notes) and `tagSuggestions(_:)`. `AppModel` keeps `tagTree`, the tagged and Untagged counts and the page on screen (`tagPage` on the Mac, `phoneTagPages` per iPhone tag screen); typing in a body that leaves its tags unchanged patches the pages in place, and a change to a file's tags redoes only the tag lists. `addTag(_:to:)` and `removeTag(_:from:)` write `tags:` through `TagEditor`; `newTask()` on a tag's page writes the tag into the new task.
- **Notes**: `Item(parsing:path:)` makes an untyped file a note (`Item.Kind.note`) when `Item.isNotePath(_:)` allows its path; `PropertyReader.readNote` reads only `createdDate` and `tags:`. `VaultIndex` keeps notes by folder (`notes`, leaving out logged files) and ranks a title's paths live Ta Da files, then notes, then logged files. `NoteQueries` gives `folderTree(folders:)` (`FolderNode`s with rolled-up counts, plus the given folders and their parents), `notes(in:)` and `looseNotes()`. The task queries, `toggleCompletion`, `setStatus` and `dateTargets` skip notes; tags, Search, the link menu and backlinks include them. `AppModel.knownFolders` holds `Vault.emptyFolders()` and the folders that held notes, found again on each load and refresh (`rescanFolders`), so a folder stays in the tree when its last note goes. `newNote()` and `newFolder(in:)` write into the shown folder; `renameFolder`, `trashFolder` (after `folderTrashConfirmation`, an alert in `ContentView`), `moveNote` and `convert` call `Vault`'s folder operations, moving notes and converting (`Storage/Notes.swift`), which rewrite links written with a moved path and return the files they relinked; `moveFiles` keeps the selection, the folder page and the open document on the moved files. `convert` (from `convertRequest`, the window sheet `ConvertSheet` that `ContentView` shows, its preview from `NoteConversion.text`) switches to Tasks and opens the file. A task's file menu opens the same sheet to make it a project, without the type switch, starting at the task's status and area, with a box to make its subtasks the project's tasks: after the move, `Vault.extractSubtasks(ofProject:)` (`SubtaskExtraction.extractAll`). The rules are in `docs/file-format.md`.
- **Integrations**: Calendar events and reminders, read only, before each day's tasks in the Agenda. TaDaCore's `ExternalEvent` and `ExternalReminder` are values with no EventKit. `DayEvent.on(_:from:calendar:)` and `byDay(_:from:through:calendar:)` place an event on each day it touches (`EventPlacement`: its start, All day, or until its end; an end at midnight doesn't reach that day), with `dayOfSpan` for an all-day event over several days, sorted all-day first, then by start and title; `ExternalReminder.today(_:today:)` takes those due today or earlier, and `upcoming(_:after:)` the later ones by date. `IntegrationsStore` (`AppModel.integrations`) keeps the Show events and Show reminders switches and the hidden calendar and list identifiers in the settings, so a new calendar or list shows, and gives each kind's `Access` (off, asking, granted, denied). Turning a switch on asks for full access to that kind. Its one `EKEventStore`, made on first use, is read off the main actor (`Fetcher`, which also lists the calendars and lists by account); a fetch a newer one overtook is dropped. `AppModel.updateIntegrations()`, with each Agenda update, sets the window from the earlier of today and the first day shown to the latest of the last day shown, Upcoming's last date and the end of next month, at most a year ahead of today (`integrationsWindowEnd(today:upcomingLast:)`); the store fetches again only when the shown range leaves the window it covers, and on `EKEventStoreChanged` and a time-zone change; `refresh()`, when the app becomes active, rereads access, turns off a switch that lost it and resets the store. The store keeps today's events and reminders, those on later days (`upcomingEvents`, `upcomingReminders`) and those on days before today (`pastEvents`, `pastReminders`: events by day, and the incomplete reminders due then). `AppModel.events(on:)` and `reminders(on:)` answer for any fetched day, with `hasEvents(on:)` for the dots. `EventRow` and `ReminderRow` draw them; an event opens Calendar (`CalendarApp`: the app on the Mac, `calshow:` on the event's day on iPhone), and neither row takes part in selection.
- **Badges**: Settings ▸ Notifications' Badges switch (`NotificationsStore`, `AppModel.notifications`, off by default) shows `AppModel.todayCount`, Today's open tasks, as the app icon's badge through `UNUserNotificationCenter.setBadgeCount`: the Dock icon on the Mac, the Home Screen icon on iPhone. `AppModel.updateLists()` passes the count, so it follows every change and the day. Turning the switch on asks for badge authorization; refused, or turned off later in System Settings (read again when the app becomes active), the switch turns off, is disabled, and Settings links to the app's notification settings. Turning it off clears the badge. The badge is set only while Ta Da runs, so on iPhone it keeps the last count until Ta Da opens again.
- **Editing**: `OpenDocument` holds the selected file's whole text. Typing, property edits and checkbox clicks change that text, which is reparsed into the index. It saves 600 ms after the last change, and also before switching files, when the app deactivates and on quit (on iOS, on entering the background, under a background task). Files load one at a time: each selection waits for the previous load, which is skipped if it hasn't started. `AppModel` does its file work off the main actor through `run`. Quitting, including by closing the window, waits for the save: `AppDelegate.applicationShouldTerminate` returns `.terminateLater`. A checkbox click in a row whose file isn't open does a coordinated read, a surgical edit and an atomic write.
- **Saving** is guarded: Ta Da writes only if the file still holds the text it last read or wrote.
- **Trash**: deleting moves a file into `.trash/` in the folder (created when needed); a project takes its linked tasks, and an area its projects, their tasks and its own tasks. Deleting an area, or files that embed images, first sets `AppModel.trashConfirmation`; with no images the Mac and iPhone show it as a question (`TrashConfirmationText`), and otherwise as `DeleteFilesDialog`, which lists each image under the first file that embeds it, ticked unless a file that stays embeds it. `confirmTrash(_:images:)` deletes, moving the ticked images to `.trash/attachments/`. `asksBeforeTrashing(_:)` tells the iPhone's menus to wait for the answer before leaving the file. `AppModel.trash` lists the Ta Da files there, reloaded on each refresh. Restoring moves files to `Ta Da/tasks/`, `Ta Da/projects/` or `Ta Da/areas/` and brings back their parents (recreated by name if deleted forever, relinked if a name changed); Delete Forever and Empty Trash move them to the system Trash (on iOS outside iCloud Drive, where that isn't supported, they delete them; the Mac never deletes); a file that can't be removed is reported and the rest still go. Attachments go to `.trash/attachments/` (`Vault.moveAttachmentsToTrash`), and restoring a file takes the trashed attachments it embeds (`TrashedAttachment.attachment(named:trashedAt:in:)`, and its embeds follow an image restored under another name), and removing a file takes those that no other trashed file or file in the folder embeds (`TrashedAttachment.attachments(of:in:others:live:)`). The rules are in `docs/file-format.md`.
- **Folder settings**: `VaultSettings` (TaDaCore) reads and writes `Ta Da/settings.json` as `docs/file-format.md` describes: per-key optionals in three groups (`DailyNotes`, `Theme`, `Autocomplete`; a template's outer optional is nil when the key is missing, its inner nil is none) and `taskLinkBoxes`, and `VaultSettings.updated(_:dailyNotes:theme:autocomplete:taskLinkBoxes:)`, which sets the keys of the groups and keys given and keeps every other key. `Vault.readSettings()`, `writeSettings(...)` and `readObsidianDailyNotes()` (`ObsidianDailyNotes`, from `.obsidian/daily-notes.json`) do the coordinated file work. `AppModel` loads the file when a folder opens, per key: the file, then this device's earlier `UserDefaults` value, then (daily folder and template) Obsidian's, then the default; an unknown theme name takes the default. A change to any of the eleven settings calls `saveSettings()`, which writes all three groups and `taskLinkBoxes` in a chain of off-main-actor tasks (`finishSavingSettings()` awaits them, and quitting does); loading never writes. `refresh()` reloads them when the file's modification date differs from the one last read or written, and not while a write is pending.
- **Logbook**: completed (Done or Cancelled) tasks and projects move into `Ta Da/logbook/<yyyy-MM>/` by their `completedDate` (`Vault.log`). The index holds logged files (`Item.isLogged`, ignoring case) so links to them resolve, after a file of the same title that isn't logged, but its task, project and area lists, the files filed under a parent, Search and the link menu leave them out, and a project's progress counts them. `AppModel.logIfDue()` logs `VaultIndex.filesToLog(completedBefore: today)` once a day (the day kept in the settings), when the folder opens and when `today` changes, after a `refresh()`; `Vault.log` skips a file no longer at its path (another device logged it). It also puts back logged files reopened elsewhere and logged projects that have open tasks (`VaultIndex.loggedProjectsWithOpenTasks`), as Doing; `logNow()` logs everything, first asking about a completed project with open tasks. `AppModel.logbook` is the logged files and the completed ones not logged yet; `LogbookGroup` groups them for the Mac's `LogbookView` and the iPhone's `PhoneLogbookScreen`. Setting a logged file's status to an open one puts it back (`Vault.unlog`) to `Ta Da/tasks/` or `Ta Da/projects/`, with its logged project as Doing; a task that's open or reopened does the same for its logged project. Completing or cancelling a project that has open tasks first sets `AppModel.completionConfirmation`, shown as `CompletionDialog` (a sheet, from `ContentView`) on the Mac and a confirmation dialog (from `PhoneRootView`) on iPhone; `answerCompletion(_:tasksAs:)` sets the project and its open tasks, and shows the next question when several projects were completed at once. The rules are in `docs/file-format.md`.
- **Recurrence**: `PropertyReader` reads a task's `repeat` as a `Repeat` (its text, and the `RepeatRule` when `RepeatRule.parse` reads it; else an `invalidRepeat` warning); a project's stays text. `RepeatRule` (`Model/RepeatRule.swift`) holds the frequency, interval, weekdays, month day or year date, `until` and `whenDone`, writes its canonical `phrase`, and gives the words the app shows (`description(until:)`, `frequencyLabel`). `RepeatRuleNext.swift` gives `next(dueDate:deadline:completedDate:rolloverDay:)`, `firstDate(from:)` and a regular rule's `dates(after:from:through:)`. `Vault.log` moves a completed repeating task forward instead of logging it, on `VaultIndex.rolloverDay` (today, or the day after for a task completed today): `Recurrence.moveForward` (`Writing/Recurrence.swift`) splices status, `completedDate`, the dates and the subtask resets in one write. `VaultIndex.repeatMove(of:today:completing:)` (`Index/RepeatMove.swift`) gives the same decision, `.moves(to:)` or `.ends`, to the status menus, rows, chips and the Logbook's Completed today. `Queries.occurrences(of:from:through:today:)` gives a task's coming `Occurrence`s (date, due or deadline, `estimated`), and `dayMarks` marks them, except for `every day` and `every weekday` rules. `AppModel.setRepeat(_:of:)` writes `repeat` (a task with no dates becomes due on the rule's first date). The due date menu (`CalendarPickerModel`, with `CalendarPickerModel.Repeating`) reads a phrase starting with `every` as a rule, shows its occurrences on the month and has the Repeat section; the iPhone's date sheet shares the model. The rules are in `docs/file-format.md`.
- **Attachments**: `Vault.taDaAttachments()` lists the images in `Ta Da/attachments/` as `Attachment`s (path, size, added date, `isDownloaded`; a placeholder's size is from its plist). `AttachmentUse.uses(of:index:trashed:imagePaths:)` finds, for each image, the live, logged and trashed files that embed it, an embed counting for the image `Vault.attachmentPath(for:in:)` resolves it to; `isInUse` is a live embedder, else `cleanUpReason` (`CleanUpReason`: `loggedOnly`, `trashedOnly`, `loggedAndTrashed`, `unused`). `Vault.attachmentGroups(index:trashed:)` returns `AttachmentGroups`: `candidates` (not in use) then `inUse`, each in Finder order by name, with their sizes and `pendingFiles` (markdown files iCloud Drive hasn't downloaded, in the folder and `.trash/`). `AppModel.attachmentGroups` is reread on each `refresh()` in the same walk as `attachments` (`reloadAttachmentGroups`, changing only when different); `attachmentEpoch` is bumped by `renameAttachment`, so a pass that walked the folder before a rename drops its result, and `renamedAttachment` tells an open iPhone image screen the new path. `attachmentSelection` holds the picked images and `attachmentNote` what the last move did; both clear when the domain changes. `attachmentUse(at:)` gives one image's use; `moveAttachmentsToTrash(_:)` (⌫, the detail pane, the iPhone's picking bar) moves unused images straight away and asks first (`ImageDialog.delete` or `.deleteSeveral`) when a live file embeds one or the use isn't known yet (just renamed); `trashAttachments(_:)` moves images to `.trash/attachments/` one at a time, reports those that failed and refreshes; `downloadAttachment(_:)` asks iCloud Drive for a placeholder. Clean Up moves its ticked images with `trashAttachments`, leaving embeds as they are. The rules are in `docs/file-format.md`.
- **Ta Da's folder**: `Vault.home` (`Ta Da`) holds the folders Ta Da creates (`Vault.taskFolder`, `projectFolder`, `areaFolder`, `attachmentFolder`, `logbookFolder`); `Item.reservedFolders` keeps it out of notes.
- **Outside changes**: `VaultWatcher` (FSEvents on the Mac; on iOS, file coordination on the folder) and app activation both trigger `refresh()`, which compares modification dates with the last scan and reparses only changed files. The open document reloads if it has no unsaved edits. With unsaved edits, `DocumentBanner` offers keep mine / use the file on disk, save again / discard after a deletion, or keep this / use the other iCloud version (`Conflicts` writes the file, marks the conflict resolved and removes the other versions in one coordinated write). Leaving the file, quitting or (on iOS) going to the background with a choice still open writes the edits to `<title> (unsaved edits).md` beside it. The open file is presented to file coordination (`FilePresenter`): another app's coordinated read or merge, iCloud Drive's uploads among them, first saves the edits, and a coordinated move or rename (Finder, iCloud) is followed. A rename without coordination (Obsidian, Terminal, git) shows as a deletion. On iOS the watcher and the open file's presenter are removed on entering the background, after the save, and added again (before the refresh) when the app becomes active, since a suspended app can't answer coordination. On iOS, files and images iCloud Drive hasn't downloaded are hidden `.Name.md.icloud` placeholders (an image's is listed in `attachments` under its real name); opening the folder and each refresh ask for them (`Vault.downloadPlaceholders()`), and each joins the index once it arrives.

## Themes

A theme set pairs a light and a dark scheme. `AppModel.theme` is the set's dark scheme when the appearance is Dark, or System while macOS is dark (`systemIsDark`, from `NSApp.effectiveAppearance`, observed with KVO), otherwise its light scheme. With a scheme per appearance on, `lightScheme` and `darkScheme` replace the set's pair. On iOS `systemIsDark` is read from the scene's `UITraitUserInterfaceStyle` (a registered trait observation), so System follows the device live. Each window's appearance follows the shown scheme, so a dark scheme in light mode still gets dark controls, context menus, alerts and popup menus: on the Mac through `preferredColorScheme`, on iOS through each window's `overrideUserInterfaceStyle`, which leaves the scene's own style, and so the trait the observation reads, as the device's; menus in the menu bar follow macOS. The theme set, the scheme-per-appearance switch and the two schemes are in the folder's settings file (see Folder settings); the appearance stays per device in `UserDefaults`.

## Quick Entry

## Updates

The Mac app updates itself with Sparkle 2.10.1, linked to the Mac only (`platformFilters`). `Updater` (`TaDa/Updates/Updater.swift`, the only file that imports Sparkle) is owned by `AppDelegate`: it starts `SPUStandardUpdaterController`, checks the feed in the background on open, and backs Ta Da ▸ Check for Updates…, which is disabled while a check runs (`canCheckForUpdates`). Sparkle also checks daily (`SUEnableAutomaticChecks`) and shows its own update window. Debug builds don't start it.

`TaDa/Info.plist` (Mac only, through `INFOPLIST_FILE[sdk=macosx*]`) holds `SUFeedURL` (`https://github.com/lobrenov/ta-da-swift/releases/latest/download/appcast.xml`), `SUPublicEDKey`, `SUEnableInstallerLauncherService` and `SUEnableAutomaticChecks`. `TaDa/TaDa.entitlements` (`CODE_SIGN_ENTITLEMENTS[sdk=macosx*]`) adds the `-spks` and `-spki` mach-lookup exceptions for Sparkle's installer; outgoing connections are on, so Sparkle downloads in the app. An update is an EdDSA-signed zip, accepted when its signature matches `SUPublicEDKey` or its code signature matches the app's. Releasing is in `docs/releases.md`.

- `PhoneIntegrations.swift`: Settings ▸ Integrations (`PhoneIntegrationsScreen`, the `integrations` route): the Show events and Show reminders switches, then the calendars and lists under each account's label as checkmark rows, or the denied state with Open Settings.
- `PhoneAttachments.swift`: `PhoneAttachmentsScreen` (the `attachments` route, from Other's Attachments row, which shows the total size): the two groups as the Mac's; a tap opens an image, "…" ▸ Select or a swipe picks several (`AttachmentsPickingBar`: Move to Trash, asking first through `PhoneDeleteImagesSheet` when a live file embeds one), and the bottom bar holds the count of images in no open file and Clean Up…. Search filters by image name or the title of a file that embeds it. `PhoneAttachmentScreen` (the `attachment(path)` route): preview, details, the embedding files (each opens its file), Share, Rename and Move to Trash; an image not downloaded is asked for on open, with Try Again on failure, and the screen follows a rename (`renamedAttachment`). `PhoneCleanUpSheet` is Clean Up's sheet.

On iOS the live editor (`LiveEditor`) uses 17 pt text; press and hold on a checkbox or callout title opens `CheckboxMenu` or `CalloutMenu` as popovers, with a medium tap. The editor scrolls the caret (and the scroll view it sits in) to stay 12 pt above the keyboard bar as it moves or the text changes.

## Editors

- **Live**: `LiveEditor` runs the engine with `.fitsContent` height inside a SwiftUI `ScrollView`, between the title and properties above and the footer (and a project's linked-tasks table) below. The engine's binding reads and writes only the body; it is spliced back after the frontmatter block. Theme tokens map to `MarkdownEditorTheme`; `TaskCheckboxStyle` takes its strikes and click transitions from `Checkbox.State` and its tints from `Theme.checkboxTint`, and `InlineCheckbox` draws the boxes; wikilinks resolve by `WikiLink.title` against the index's `titles`; a link to a task draws its status box, from the file a click opens (`VaultIndex.taskStatuses`), unless `AppModel.taskLinkBoxes` (Settings ▸ Editor ▸ Links, on by default, kept in the folder's settings file) is off. A click on a link (on iPhone a tap, unless the caret touches it) opens its file: on the Mac a project or area as its page, and a task in its project's or area's page or in Loose tasks (`AppModel.openLink(to:)`); on iPhone the file is pushed. Renaming a file points the links to it in other files' bodies at the new title (`Vault.retargetLinks`), the open file's in place. The properties' meta line ends with “N backlinks”, a menu of the files that link here; the Mac's footer shows when the file was modified. Its keyboard focus is the engine's `isFocused` binding: `takesFocus` sets it (caret at the body's start, then `onTookFocus`), and on iPhone the keyboard bar's hide keyboard clears it.
- **Tags in the editor**: with `MarkdownEditorConfiguration.tags` set (a `TagStyle`), the engine parses `#tags` by TaDaCore's rules, outside code, links and headings' markers, and draws each as a bubble in inline code's shape: `accent` at 14% fill and 32% ring (10% and 28% on light schemes), `#` at 65%. On the caret's line tags are plain source. A click (on iPhone a tap) on a bubble calls `onTagClick`, which shows the tag's page on the Mac and pushes its screen on iPhone.
- **Source**: `SourceTextView`, a TextKit 1 `NSTextView` with a line-number ruler and a current-line highlight. It edits the whole file, frontmatter included. `SourceHighlighter` (in TaDaCore, on `FrontmatterSplitter`, `WikiLink` and `LocalDate`) colours it by role (key `accent`, value `fg`, string `done`, date `due`, heading `strong`, punctuation and checkbox `muted`, done checkbox `done`, link `link`) and reruns over the whole file after each edit.
- **Autocomplete**: `AppModel.autocomplete` holds Settings ▸ Editor's switches (Brackets, Text formatting, Wikilinks and embeds, Tags, all on by default), kept in the folder's settings file. `LiveEditor` passes them to the engine's `ListStyle`. A typed `(`, `[` or `{`, a formatting marker (`*`, `**`, `_`, `__`, `~~`, `==`, `` ` ``) or `[[` adds its closer, and typing that closer steps over it while the caret stays between the pair. Markers pair only between words and outside code; a single `*` at a line's start stays a bullet, a space straight after a marker removes its closer, and a run past the longest marker (`***`, ```` ``` ````, `~~~`, `===`) stays unpaired.
- **Slash menu**: `/` in the Live editor opens `SlashMenu`, a themed list of `SlashCommand` formats in a `PopupMenu`: a borderless panel that places itself under its anchor and closes on a click elsewhere or a scroll. The checkbox, file and property menus use it too.
- **Images**: `LiveEditor` gives the engine `AttachmentImages`, which finds an embed's image among `AppModel.attachments` (the folder's images, reread on each refresh) as `Vault.attachmentPath(for:in:)` does and decodes it at up to 2048 pixels. An image alone on its line draws at its pixel size in points, fitted to the editor's width, with rounded corners, and stays drawn as the caret passes; the caret steps over its hidden `![[ ]]`, and ⌫ after it removes the embed. On the Mac a `</>` button over a hovered image ("Show markdown") shows the source until the caret leaves the line. A missing image's name is muted at 60% with a dashed underline. A pasted image, or an image file dropped from Finder on the Mac, goes through `AppModel.embed(_:)`: image data is saved as `Ta Da/attachments/Pasted image <time>.png` (on iPhone in its own format), a file from outside the folder is copied into `Ta Da/attachments/` keeping its name (numbered against that folder), and an image already in the folder is embedded where it is. `embed(_:)` returns the embed straight away, from the names `attachments` already holds, and writes the file off the main actor (`savingImage`). On the Mac only the image types in `Vault.imageExtensions` paste as files; another image file pastes as PNG data. The embed goes on its own line.
- **Embed menu** (`EmbedMenu.swift`): on the Mac a click selects an image (an accent ring) and a right-click opens Copy, Cut, Rename…, Reveal in Finder and Delete in the file menu's panel (`FileMenu.show(_:at:in:theme:onClose:)`); ⌘C, ⌘X and ⌘⌫ do the same on a selected image. On iPhone a press and hold opens Copy, Cut, Edit Markdown, Rename…, Show in Files and Delete. Copy puts the image file (which a paste in Ta Da embeds as it is) and its data, read off the main actor, on the pasteboard; Cut also removes the `![[ ]]` as one undo step. Rename and Delete set `AppModel.imageDialog` (`.rename`, `.delete`, or `.deleteSeveral` from Attachments), shown as sheets by `ImageDialogs.swift`. `renameAttachment(_:to:)` renames the file in its folder, keeping its extension and refusing another image's name, and retargets every embed that showed it (`Vault.retargetEmbeds`), the open file's in place. `trashAttachment(_:)` moves the image to `.trash/attachments/`; its embeds stay and show as missing. Trash lists each trashed file's images under it (`trashedAttachmentsByFile`, the images that go with it alone), picked with it, then the other trashed images (`looseTrashedAttachments`), with the files whose embeds are missing them. Image thumbnails (`ImageThumbnail`, in the dialogs, Trash and the delete questions) decode off the main actor into `ThumbnailCache`, which the link menu's rows share.
- **Link menu**: `[[` opens `LinkMenuController`'s `PopupMenu` under it, on the folder's files; `![[` on its images (`Vault.attachmentPaths()`, read once per query). The engine reports both as slash queries with a `link` or `embed` trigger; `BodyEditor` passes a `LinkMenuSource` (the index, the folder, `AppModel.recentFiles` and the open file, which is left out). `LinkMenuSearch` ranks by `LinkSearch`: an empty `[[` lists the last eight files opened (every file by title when none has been), and a query searches the recent ones first, then the rest by title. A pick replaces the query and its `]]` with `[[Title]]` or `![[name.png]]` as one undo step, with the caret after it. `AppModel.recentFiles` keeps nine paths in the settings, follows renames, and adds each file as it opens.
- **Tag menu** (tagAutocompleteMenu): with Autocomplete's Tags on, `#` after whitespace or at a line's start, then a character that isn't a digit or `/`, opens a query the engine reports with a `tag` trigger; any character that can't be in a tag ends it, keeping the text. `LinkMenuSource.tagSuggestions(_:)` lists `VaultIndex.tagSuggestions` (a segment starting with the query; first-segment matches, then by use, then A–Z, up to 50), leaving out tags only the open file's body carries once. On the Mac `TagMenuController` shows them in a compact `PopupMenu` under the `#`; on iPhone `PhoneTagList` shows them above the keyboard bar. ↵ or Tab (a tap on iPhone) writes `#path ` in place of the query; with no match the menu says "New tag".
- **Tables**: on the Mac `LiveEditor` sets `liveTables` and `tableAccent` (`accent`), so a table is a grid of cells over its lines (`MarkdownTable`, `TableGridView`, `TableCellEditor` in MarkdownEngine) instead of its `|` source, and the caret never enters those lines. Cells show the editor's markdown but embeds and tables; the one being edited has the keyboard. Each edit rewrites the table's lines padded as one splice (`docs/file-format.md`) and one document undo step. Tab, Shift-Tab and the arrow keys move between cells and out of the table; a hovered row or column shows a grip that selects it (⌘C, ⌘X, ⌫, esc) or drags it to move it, the header staying first. The grips' right-click menus are `RightClickMenu`'s (`RightClickMenuController`, from the engine's `onRightClickMenu`): insert, delete and a column's alignment. Columns take their natural width up to the editor's width, then share it. iPhone keeps the rendered image and revealed source.
- **Checkbox menu**: a right-click on a Live editor checkbox opens `CheckboxMenu`, the same kind of panel listing every marker (subtasks, then icons) with its box drawn by `InlineCheckbox`; on a subtask with text, Extract to Task sits between them. While open it takes ↑↓, ↵ and esc.
- **Extract to Task** (`ExtractTask`): the line under the checkbox (`NSTextView.characterIndex(for:)` at the box) or the caret (Task ▸ Extract to Task) gives a `SubtaskExtraction.Extract`; `AppModel.extractTask` creates the task, then the line and the lines indented under it are deleted as one undo step, if unchanged meanwhile. `AppModel.extracted` shows `ExtractedToast` at the document's foot for 6 seconds, with Open. The rules are in `docs/file-format.md`.
- **Callouts**: `LiveEditor` gives the engine a `CalloutStyle`, so `> [!type]` quotes draw as panels (types and aliases from `CalloutType`, icons from `CalloutIcon`). A click on a foldable callout's icon, chevron or the row past its title folds or opens it on screen only; the file keeps its `+` or `-`, and a folded body shows while the caret is in it. A right-click on a title line opens `CalloutMenu` under the icon, with the callout ringed: picking a type or fold edits only the text between `[!` and `]` or the character after it, as one undo step. While open it takes ↑↓, ↵ and esc.
- **Properties**: `PropertyChips` shows status, the parent (a task's project or area, a project's area; struck through when unresolved), dueDate and deadline as chips under the title (an area has none; a task's repeat rule follows its due date, or its deadline when it has no due date), and the type, createdDate and completedDate in a line below. A chip opens its picker under it through `PropertyPicker`. Status, move and area are `PopupMenu`s: they take ↑↓, ↵, esc and typed letters, and the move and area menus' search fields take the keyboard. The move menu lists Loose tasks, loose projects, then each area followed by its projects; the area menu No area and the areas. For several files a menu ticks a row only when they all agree. New Project and New Area create the file and file the tasks under it (`AppModel.newProject(named:linking:)`, `newArea(named:filing:)`); Create Area makes the area an unresolved link names. A date opens `CalendarPicker`, a month in a `PopupPanel` under a field that has the keyboard: a typed date moves the cursor to its day, ↵ sets the cursor's day, and esc empties the field, then closes. VoiceOver gets each chip's choices as actions on the chip.
- **Tags row**: under the property chips on task, project and area pages (and their browser headers), `TagChipRow` shows the file's `tags:` as filled chips (× on hover removes one), + Tag, then "In text" and the body's tags as outlined chips. A chip click shows the tag's page. + Tag, or ⌘T and Task ▸ Tags… (`AppModel.showTagMenu()` sets `propertyMenuRequest`; the open file's Tags row, in a page or a browser header, opens it under + Tag), or the file menu's Tags… opens tagMenu (`PropertyPicker`, a `PopupMenu` with a search field): In this file (`tags:` ticked, body tags greyed "in text"), then All tags with counts, or the matches with Create #name first when no tag has that path. A pick adds or removes the tag and the menu stays open. Task rows show a `tag` glyph (`RowGlyphs`), whose hover lists the tags and where each is written.

## Obsidian

The Obsidian plugin (id `ta-da`) is its own repo, `ta-da-obsidian`, cloned beside this one (`~/Projects/ta-da-obsidian`). Its `src/core/` is a TypeScript port of TaDaCore's model, parsing, writing, index and queries, and of the editor fork's `MarkdownEngineSyntax`; it is kept in step by the conformance fixtures: `ConformanceExportTests` writes DesignVault's and `HomeVault`'s parse and query results, the write cases, and the rules, next dates, occurrences and `dayMarks` of `RecurrenceVault` (`recurrence.json`), and `LoggingVault`'s files to log, logging and putting back, and the daily run in sequence and again (`logging.json`) to `Packages/TaDaCore/Tests/TaDaCoreTests/Fixtures/Conformance/`, and the plugin's tests read them there.

It reads the vault's markdown into its own `VaultIndex` as `docs/file-format.md` says, and reads `.trash/` for its Trash list. It writes by splicing lines, as the apps do: the open file through Obsidian's editor, other files through `Vault.process`, renames through `FileManager.renameFile`, deletes through `vault.trash(file, false)`. It creates files in `Ta Da/` and daily and week notes in the folders its own settings name (`Ta Da/settings.json`, which the Obsidian plugin shares for `dailyNotes`), and applies the apps' status rules, moving a reopened logged file out of the Logbook. It logs as the apps do, as one more app on the folder: once a day per device, when the layout is ready and when the day changes, it logs the files completed before today, puts back reopened logged files and logged projects with open tasks, and keeps the day in its `data.json` only when the run succeeds; Log now in the Logbook logs every completed file. Each move picks its name when it runs and skips a file another app has already moved or reopened. It reads `repeat` rules and gives their next dates, occurrences and `dayMarks`, and its logging moves completed repeating tasks forward. Its recurrence UI follows the Mac app's: rules typed in the due date modal (Repeat section, Ends view), the repeat chip suffix, "↻ <date>" on done and skipped rows, "Skip this time" and "moves to <date>" in the status menu, the Agenda's Repeats group, and a Logbook that leaves out tasks that will move forward (`repeatMove`, a port of `RepeatMove`).

It extracts a subtask to a task and converts a task to a project as the apps do (`SubtaskExtraction`, `NoteConversion`). Its own items join Obsidian's file menu (Move…, Schedule…, Set deadline…, Tags…, Status…, Convert to project…) and files menu, which are also a navigator row's right-click menu; in its views, a project's or area's menu adds Delete project or Delete area, which follow the apps' Deleting rules without the attachments question. Rows are picked with ⌘-click and Shift-click, and dragged onto a project or area through Obsidian's `dragManager`, on desktop. Extract to task is in the editor's right-click menu.

On a phone, the navigator in Obsidian's drawer shows one pane at a time, with a top bar (back, Search, New task) and a bottom bar for Agenda and Projects.

It doesn't show Calendar events and reminders, and its Trash is read only. Its layout, commands and rules are in its `CLAUDE.md`; what it does for users is in its `README.md`.
